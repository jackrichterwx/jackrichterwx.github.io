Research Highlights

We used a self-organizing map (SOM) framework with 9 nodes to classify and evaluate snowfall events in Marquette, MI (Wagner et al. 2026). We discovered that each node had different composite synoptic, macrophysical, and microphysical properties and that the 9 nine nodes could be grouped into synoptically-forced, lake-effect, or ambiguous regimes. The figure below shows radar reflectivity to snowfall rate (Z-S) relations for each of the 9 nodes with clear differences observed between the synoptic (blue), lake-effect (red), and ambiguous (black) nodes.




We examined snowfall events coincident with an atmospheric river (AR Snowfall) against snowfall events not coincident with an atmospheric river (NoAR snowfall) in Marquette, MI (MQT). This study revealed that there are large-scale and microphysical differences between AR and NoAR snowfall (Richter et al. 2025). The figure below highlights N0-lambda particle size distribution (PSD) parameterizations filled by various mean properties of each bin. AR snowfall is more intense and is associated with warmer temperatures and a higher number concentration of particles compared to NoAR snowfall.
